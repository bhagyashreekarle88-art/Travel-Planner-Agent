# Goa
## Overview
Goa is India's smallest state, on the Konkan coast, known for beaches, Portuguese-era churches and forts, seafood and a relaxed nightlife scene. North Goa (Calangute, Baga, Anjuna, Vagator) is busier and party-oriented; South Goa (Palolem, Colva, Agonda) is quieter and better for families. Panaji is the compact capital on the Mandovi river.
## Best Time to Visit
November to February is peak season with dry, pleasant weather and the highest hotel prices. March to May is hot and humid. June to September is the monsoon: very heavy rain, rough seas, beach shacks close and water sports stop, but hotels are much cheaper and the hinterland is lush green.
## Neighbourhoods
Calangute and Baga suit first-timers who want nightlife. Candolim is calmer with mid-range resorts. Anjuna and Vagator attract backpackers and cafe culture. Panaji (Panjim) is best for heritage walks and food. Palolem and Colva in the south suit couples and families.

## Food
Try fish curry rice, prawn balchão, chicken xacuti, bebinca (layered dessert) and Goan sausages. Beach shacks are best for seafood in season; Panaji has heritage restaurants.
## Getting Around
Rent a scooter (with a valid licence and helmet) or a self-drive car; app cabs are limited, so local taxis and GoaMiles are common. Distances between North and South Goa can take 1.5 to 2 hours.
## Travel Tips
Carry cash for shacks and markets. Respect lifeguard flags; swimming is unsafe in the monsoon. Dress modestly when visiting churches.
## Safety and Entry
No permits needed for Indian citizens. Drink driving is strictly penalised.
